On Paper · identity refinement · six marks, twenty-eight variations

The circle and the vertical event

You picked six marks and they turned out to be one skeleton: a circle with a vertical event beside it, inside it, or cut out of it. So this round is depth, not territory — each parent pushed four ways, with every optical correction stated as a number, plus four marks that stopped choosing between them and fused instead.

Colophon — four ways Open Counter — four ways Spine — four ways Restated — four ways Margin — four ways Loose Leaf — four ways Convergence

Where this lands

The constraint did the work. Each parent got a variation whose only job was to defeat its own flagged weakness — chiefly that a circle beside a vertical bar is Patreon's old mark — and three of the four best things here came out of that instruction. The refinements that merely tuned interval and weight are better drawings of the parent, and their designers say plainly that they do not escape the silhouette.

Eclipse

The ring crosses into the stem with a paper channel between them, so the two stop being neighbours and become one interlocked figure. It reads as a P, and numerals never overlap — which kills both the '10' reading and the prior art at once.

One Stroke

The monoline resolved into a single continuous p. The most drawn thing in the set and the furthest from anything constructed out of two objects.

One Circle

The circle split down its own axis — solid on one side, ring on the other. The most original mark of the whole exploration, and the only one with no letter in it at all: brave or a miss, depending on how much you need the P.

Echo

Margin's interiority with Spine's bow: a heavy ring holding a curved coral rule. The bow is the one gesture in this family that no geometric prior art owns.

Display Cut

Display Cut, with Caption Cut as its 16px counterpart below. The only submission that treats small sizes as a different drawing rather than the same drawing shrunk — which is what shipping on four platforms actually needs.

Initial

The safe one: a solid geometric P with a hairline gap that closes below about 48px. Most legible, least distinctive — worth keeping on the table as the conservative option.

Two honest cautions. The interval-and-weight variations — Counterweight, Close Interval, Interval, Open Page — are better-drawn parents but still a bar beside a circle; their own designers say so in the risk lines. And Standing O, Full Stop and Embrace each solved their brief by losing the thread: a stacked disc and ring reads as a figure, and a crescent with a dot reads as a moon.

Colophon — four ways

Colophon — the heavy bar and heavy ring. Flagged for reading as the numeral 10, and for the bar-and-circle prior art.

Colophon round one

A solid vertical ink lozenge (140 wide, 540 tall, fully rounded ends) and, 90 units to its right with their tops exactly aligned, a heavy disc of outer radius 200 with a small 120-unit-wide round counter punched through its centre; the bar descends 140 units below the disc's bottom, so the eye closes a bold uppercase P.

This is the first pinned reference restated in On Paper's own materials — heavy black on warm paper, a printer's-mark seriousness that matches 'substance, clearly expressed'. The tiny counter keeps it at the P end of the territory's range: mass first, letter second.

Risk It reads as the numeral 10 (or 'IO') at least as readily as a deconstructed P — a stranger may see a bar and a record before a letter. The counter (~2px) and the gap (~1.4px) both survived my 16px downscale test but are at the absolute limit; a poor rasteriser could fuse the gap and turn it into a plain bold P. It does not read as a page, a fold, or a font letterform.

The parent held at arm's length and re-weighted: a heavier stem (152) against a lighter ring (stroke 108) whose counter has opened to 184, outer diameter 400 held, gap widened to 104.

Changed Gap 90 to 104; bar width 140 to 152; ring stroke 140 to 108; counter 120 to 184 (outer 400 held); descent below disc 140 held plus cap compensation. This is the ratio I believe in: stem-to-ring at roughly root-2 (152 against a visual 104) follows how type actually works — the stem is the heavy stroke, the bowl lightens — so the pair reads as one weighted letterform rather than two equal objects, and equal weights ARE the Patreon and numeral-10 case. The counter at 184 (2.9px at 16px) and the gap at 104 (1.6px) both clear the two limits the parent was flagged for.

Risk In silhouette this is still, honestly, a bar beside a ring — a lazy describer can still say Patreon or 10. The weight flip and open counter argue the case at arm's length, not from across the room; this variation refines the parent rather than escaping its prior art.

Eclipse kill-the-risk

The two objects stop being neighbours and interlock: the ring passes in front of the stem, its outer edge crossing 36 units into the bar, separated by a 36-unit paper channel — a seal pinned over a stem, not a circle beside a bar.

Changed Gap 90 to minus 36 (overlap: ring outer edge at x=402 crosses into the bar, whose right edge is 438); a new 36-unit paper channel realised as a ground-colour stroke of 196 under the 124 ink ring (halo radii 40-236 against ink radii 76-200); bar 140 to 168 wide; counter 120 to 152; ring stroke 140 to 124; stem remnant at the ring's equator verified at 96 units (1.5px at 16px). Optical corrections: ring top overshoots bar top by 6 (from the parent's equal tops); ring stroke 124 = visual target 120 + 4 (+3.3%) curve compensation; descender 140 to 146 (+6) round-cap compensation; vertical centroid at y=495, 17 (1.7%) above geometric centre; shifted -24 from pure centroid-centring so bbox-centre (536) and centroid (488) straddle x=512. This is the variation that cannot be described as a circle and a bar: the shapes share space, so the silhouette is one interlocked figure. It kills both flagged readings at once — 10/IO dies because numerals never overlap, and the Patreon silhouette dies because Patreon's elements are disjoint and its circle is solid where this ring is open.

And the knockout-interlock move has prior art of its own (chain-link and monogram traditions), so it escapes Patreon without being wholly unprecedented. It no longer resembles the bar-and-circle prior art; that claim I can make plainly.

Open Counter — four ways

Open Counter — the monoline version. Flagged for the same silhouette, and for a coral stroke that thins to about 1px at 16px.

Open Counter round one

Everything one 72-unit monoline: a vertical coral rule (72 by 560, rounded ends) and, 110 units to its right with tops aligned, a monoline ring of outer radius 216 whose counter is 288 units wide; the rule descends 128 units below the ring, which is the only thing still whispering P.

This is the interval the brand guide explicitly asks for: at this counter size the mark stops being a P and becomes an O with a rule beside it — and On Paper is an O and a P. Coral monoline is the closest of the three to the user's outlined-coral reference, and the quietest, most contemporary drawing.

Risk The O-dominance cuts both ways: first readings may be '10', 'IO', or a power symbol beside a line rather than any letter at all. The 72-unit coral stroke drops to about 1px at 16px and is the faintest of the three against warm paper — it passed the downscale test but with the least margin. No fold, document, or font-letterform reading.

Spine — the bowed stroke. Its bow is the one gesture with no prior art, and it flattened to nothing at icon size.

Risk Enumerated element by element it is still a circle and a vertical bar, so it has not left the neighbourhood — but Patreon's bar stands full-height BESIDE its circle, and nobody describes that mark as 'a circle with a line inside it'; containment plus the 2:1 weight contrast puts this a genuine distance away.
