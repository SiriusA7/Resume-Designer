# Round two — depth on six marks

Read `BRIEF.md` first for the product, the palette and the hard constraints. They
all still bind. This file is what changed.

## What happened

Round one drew eighteen marks across six territories. The client chose six:
**Colophon**, **Open Counter**, **Spine**, **Restated**, **Margin** and
**Loose Leaf**.

Look at that list before you draw anything, because the choice is more coherent
than the six territories it came from. Every single pick is **a circle and a
rule**:

- Colophon — a heavy bar beside a heavy ring
- Open Counter — a monoline rule beside a monoline ring
- Spine — a bowed stroke beside a ring
- Restated — a circle stated twice, as disc and as ring
- Margin — a rule set *inside* the ring
- Loose Leaf — the bar and the bowl cut *out of* a disc

So the identity is already decided in outline: **an O with a vertical event**.
Round two is not a search. It is the part where a mark is actually won or lost —
weight, interval, counter size, overshoot, and the honest question of whether
the thing is distinct enough to belong to somebody.

## The problem you must solve

**A circle beside a vertical bar is Patreon's old mark.** Loose Leaf's own
designer flagged it; it applies just as much to Colophon and Open Counter. Four
of the six picks sit in that silhouette. If this identity ships as a plain
bar-and-circle it will be described as "like Patreon" by somebody in the first
week, and that is a real cost for a product whose whole pitch is being
distinctly, carefully made.

You are not being asked to abandon the idea. You are being asked to find the
version of it that is unmistakably its own. The levers that actually move it:

- **Interval.** Patreon's bar and circle sit apart at a conversational distance.
  Close the gap until they nearly touch, or open it until the bar is clearly a
  margin rule and the circle is clearly a page, and you are somewhere else.
- **Weight ratio.** Equal-weight bar and ring is the generic case. A hairline
  rule against a heavy ring — or the reverse — is a decision.
- **The counter.** A small counter makes a P-ish mass; a large counter makes an
  O with a rule. The tipping point between them is the most interesting number
  in this whole exercise.
- **Height and alignment.** A bar aligned top-and-bottom with the circle reads
  as two objects. A bar that overshoots, descends, or starts below the circle's
  top reads as a letter or as a margin.
- **The bow.** Spine's curve is the one gesture in the set that no geometric
  prior art has. It is currently too shy to survive small sizes.
- **Cutting instead of drawing.** Loose Leaf's figure-ground inversion is
  already a step away from the prior art. Push it.

## Optical correction — the part nobody does

These marks are built from circles and rectangles, which means every one of them
is currently *mathematically* aligned and therefore *optically* wrong. Fix it:

- **A circle must overshoot a flat edge to look level with it.** A ring whose
  top sits at the same y as a bar's top will look short by roughly 1.5–2% of its
  diameter. Overshoot it and say by how much.
- **A curved stroke looks thinner than a straight one of the same width.** If a
  ring and a bar are both 88 units, the ring reads lighter. Thicken the curve by
  2–4% or thin the bar.
- **Optical centre is above geometric centre.** A mark centred at y=512 sits
  low; most identities shift up by 1–2% of the canvas.
- **Round caps read shorter than flat ones.** A round-capped bar of the same
  length looks stubbier than a flat-capped one.

State every correction you apply as a number, and state what it was corrected
from. A refinement round with no numbers in it did not happen.

## What each of you is doing

You are given ONE parent mark. Read its file. Keep its identity — someone should
see your four and say "those are all versions of that". Draw four:

1. **Interval and weight.** Hold the concept, move the two numbers that define
   it, and pick the ratio you actually believe in.
2. **Toward the letter.** Push it until it is unmistakably a P.
3. **Toward the O.** Push it until it is unmistakably an O with a vertical
   event — the calmer, more abstract end.
4. **Kill the risk.** Your parent has a flagged weakness, quoted in your
   instructions. This variation exists only to defeat it. If the weakness is the
   Patreon silhouette, this is the one that must look like nobody else's.

Every file is a complete app icon: a 1024 ground with the mark on it, exactly as
round one delivered them, so they can be compared without re-staging.

Name your files `<parent>-r2-<n>.svg` where `<parent>` is the parent's id.
